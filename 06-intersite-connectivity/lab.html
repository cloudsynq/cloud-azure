VNet Peering — Connect Two Isolated Networks

Build two VNets · Prove they can't talk · Peer them · Prove they can

What We'll Build

Two virtual networks that live in complete isolation. We'll place a Linux VM in each, prove that they cannot reach each other, then set up VNet Peering and prove that they can. No VPN gateway, no extra cost — just peering.

Resource Group: rg-peering-lab │ ├── VNet-A (10.1.0.0/16) East US │ └── Subnet-A 10.1.0.0/24 │ └── VM-A (private IP: 10.1.0.4) │ └── VNet-B (10.2.0.0/16) East US └── Subnet-B 10.2.0.0/24 └── VM-B (private IP: 10.2.0.4) BEFORE PEERING: VM-A ✗ cannot ping VM-B AFTER PEERING: VM-A ✓ can ping VM-B

Key Facts Before Starting

  • No overlapping address spaces — 10.1.x.x and 10.2.x.x are different, so peering is allowed.
  • Peering is not transitive — if A peers with B and B peers with C, A cannot reach C automatically.
  • Two connections are always made — one from A→B and one from B→A. Both must exist.
  • No reboot, no downtime — peering takes effect in seconds.

Configure VNet Peering
Steps 10–11 · Two peering links required
Why Two Links?
Azure peering is not automatic in both directions. When you go to VNet-A and add a peer, the portal can optionally add the return link (VNet-B → VNet-A) at the same time. Make sure "Add a peering link to [VNet-B]" checkbox is checked — otherwise you need to add the second link manually from VNet-B.

7
Optional — Test the Transitive Routing Limitation
Step 14 · Concept demonstration (no extra resources needed)
14
Why peering is NOT transitive

Imagine a third VNet-C. Even if:

  • VNet-A ↔ VNet-B (peered)
  • VNet-B ↔ VNet-C (peered)

Azure will NOT allow VM-A in VNet-A to reach VM-C in VNet-C. The route does not exist.

To allow A ↔ C, you must add a direct peering between VNet-A and VNet-C.

When Does This Matter?
In hub-and-spoke architecture (a common enterprise pattern), the hub VNet peers with every spoke. If spoke-1 needs to talk to spoke-2, the traffic goes through the hub using a firewall/NVA — it does NOT go directly from spoke-1 to spoke-2 without a route table. Azure peering alone cannot do it.
